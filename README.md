# GCC-ASME-Robot-2027
Germanna Community College ASME Competition Robot C++ Code

*** ASME Robot - Code Relevant Details ***

[7.3 Power, energy, and control]
7.3-1 The Robot runs on electricity from onboard batteries, plus the pneumatics and stored
mechanical energy allowed below. No internal-combustion engines, fuel, or
combustion of any kind.
7.3-5 Teams supply their own control system. The Robot uses one control link: either a
tether or a single wireless link. Wireless control must use only license-free consumer
bands (such as 2.4 GHz or 5 GHz) at legal power. The team is responsible for its link
not interfering with the venue or another team, and for being able to disable the Robot
on request. A team whose link interferes must resolve it or be disabled. If a tether is
used, it must remain completely slack and trailing behind the Robot, and it may not be
connected to any stationary object or person. It is not part of the Robot, is not counted
for weight, and is not measured for height in The Ascent. Any tension observed pulling,
lifting, or steadying the Robot is a violation and draws a Minor Penalty; wireless control
is encouraged. The safety line an Official attaches for The Ascent (Rule 7.4-3) is not a
tether under this rule. Autonomous control may use an onboard processor or the
stored-energy methods of Rule 7.3-3.

[8. Inspection]
8-3 Repairs and re-inspection. Between Runs, teams may make minor repairs and
adjustments at their team table, including replacing broken parts with equivalents and
changing code on a laptop. Major modifications are not allowed: a major modification
is any change to the Robot's fit, form, or function. Any change that could affect the
Robot's compliance with Sections 7, 9, or 10, including its size, weight, or safety state,
requires re-inspection before the Robot competes again. Teams are expected to
maintain the spirit of the competition and not perform a complete redesign between
Runs. Final determination of compliance rests with the Inspector; disputes are
resolved by the Head Judge. The laptop is not part of the Robot and may not control or
assist the Robot during a Run beyond the Operator Console.

Game Objects are common, inexpensive, globally available items, chosen to be safe to handle
and to be struck by a Robot. A shared supply is provided at each Station.

11.3 How a Run works
11.3-1 Every Run is 2 minutes 30 seconds: a 30-second Autonomous Phase then a 2-minute
Crewed Phase. The Sprint and The Ascent use this same window; The Sprint is
scored on its finish time within the window, and The Ascent is scored at the final
buzzer.
11.3-2 The Robot is brought to the Station inside its container. Before a Run, the Crew
removes the Robot from the container and places it in the Discipline's Starting Zone on
its own, within the 40 cm starting size, then steps back. At the start signal, one Crew
member may press a single start control on the Robot or Operator Console to begin
the Autonomous Phase, then must step away. A Robot that plans no Autonomous
Phase action simply remains still until the Crewed Phase.
11.3-3 During the Autonomous Phase no one may touch the Robot or the Operator Console
or send any input after the start control. The Robot runs on its own program or stored
energy. Breaking this voids any Autonomous Phase bonus and may draw a penalty.
11.3-4 When the Crewed Phase begins, the Crew operates the Robot through the Operator
Console.
11.4 The Crew
11.4-1 For each Run a team may bring up to four Crew members to the Field. The Crew may
change between Runs but must always be registered members of that team.
11.4-2 Only Crew members may operate the Operator Console, handle the Robot when
permitted, or speak with Officials at the Field during the Run. Other team members are
welcome to cheer and call out from outside the Field; that spirit is encouraged.
Mentors and other non-team adults may not operate the Robot or coach during a Run.

[12. The Five Disciplines]
(1) 12.1 The Sprint
Objective. Cross a course of Gates and Hurdles as fast as possible. Worth up to 100 points.
Field and layout
A lane 2.0 m wide and 7.0 m long, with a start line and a finish line 7.0 m apart. Four Gates,
each a 60 cm wide opening, are spaced about every 1.4 m along the lane on alternating sides,
forming a slalom. Two Hurdles, each 60 cm wide and 5 cm tall, are placed on alternating sides
of the lane rather than spanning it, so a Robot may either cross over a Hurdle or pass it on the
open side. The Starting Zone is at the start line.
How it is played
The clock runs from the start signal until the Robot fully crosses the finish line, up to the 2
minute 30 second Run. The four Gates must be taken in order, on alternating sides, and both
Hurdles should be crossed by driving over them. Missing a Gate, going around a Hurdle, and
any Reset each add a penalty, as below. If the Robot passes through the first Gate on its own
during the Autonomous Phase, the team earns a 10 point bonus. A Robot that does not cross
the finish line within the Run scores 0 for The Sprint.
(2) 12.2 The Lift
Objective. Place calibrated Dumbbells onto a three-tier podium. Heavier Dumbbells higher up
score more. Worth up to 120 points.
Field and layout
Within the 3.6 m by 3.6 m Field, a podium with three flat tiers at 20 cm, 40 cm, and 60 cm
stands against the far edge. Each tier has a top surface about 40 cm by 40 cm. A loading zone
holding the Dumbbell supply (at least six of each mass) sits 1.0 m in front of the podium. The
Starting Zone is behind the loading zone.
How it is played
The Robot carries Dumbbells from the loading zone and places them on the podium. A
Dumbbell scores if, at the final buzzer, it is resting on a tier and supported by the podium rather
than by the Robot. Dumbbells may be stacked; every Dumbbell in a stable stack scores, and the
whole stack is counted at the tier its bottom Dumbbell rests on. A stack must be stable: if any
Dumbbell in a stack would fall were the Robot not present, the entire stack does not score.
Dumbbells placed during the Autonomous Phase are worth 1.5 times their value.
(3) 12.3 The Cycle
Objective. Collect Balls and score them through goals. This is the basis of the playoff. Worth up
to 140 points.
Field and layout
Within the 3.6 m by 3.6 m Field, a Low Goal with a 40 cm wide floor-level opening and a High
Goal with a 30 cm opening centered at 1.0 m stand on the far edge. A Ball supply sits at a
loading area about 1.8 m from the goals. The Starting Zone is at the loading area. Both goals
are pass-through: a Ball counts when it passes fully through the opening.
How it is played
The Robot collects Balls and scores them through the goals. A Ball counts when it passes fully
through a goal opening. There is no limit on how many a Robot may score, though the
maximum caps the points. A Robot may control at most three Balls at a time; deliberately
herding or pushing more than three draws a Minor Penalty. Balls scored during the Autonomous
Phase count double.

(4) 12.4 The Marksman
Objective. Land Bean Bags on a ringed Target as accurately as possible, with a Loader feeding
one Bean Bag at a time. Worth up to 160 points.
Field and layout
Within the 3.6 m by 3.6 m Field, a flat ring Target, 60 cm across with five concentric rings, lies
on the floor with its center 2.5 m from the No-Cross Zone line. This distance is too far to place a
Bean Bag by reaching across, so Bean Bags must be launched, tossed, or slid. The rings score,
from the bullseye outward, 20, 16, 12, 8, and 4 points. A loading station holding the eight Bean
Bags sits behind the No-Cross Zone, where the Starting Zone is.
How it is played
The Robot delivers Bean Bags onto the Target from behind the No-Cross Zone. Parts of the
Robot touching the floor must stay behind the line; an arm or launcher may reach over it in the
air. To reward driving, the Robot may start a Run holding at most one Bean Bag, and during the
Crewed Phase the Loader hands Bean Bags to the Robot one at a time. During the
Autonomous Phase no one may touch the Robot or Bean Bags; the Robot may, on its own,
launch the Bean Bag it started with and also collect additional Bean Bags from the loading
station and launch those, all without human help. As a stricter, discipline-specific exception to
Rule 7.3-3, any launching mechanism must start each Run in its unloaded state; pre-tensioned
launchers are not allowed in this Discipline. A Bean Bag scored during the Autonomous Phase
counts double.

(5) 12.5 The Ascent
Objective. Climb the wall. The higher the Robot is holding at the buzzer, the more it scores, and
climbing on its own is worth much more. Worth up to 180 points.
Field and layout
Within the 3.6 m by 3.6 m Field, a vertical climbing wall at least 2.2 m tall stands against the far
edge, fitted with rungs, holds, and bars, with a padded base. Three Height Bands are marked at
0.5 m, 1.0 m, and 1.5 m, measured from the floor to the lowest point of the Robot. The Summit
is reached when the lowest point of the Robot is held at 2.0 m or higher with nothing touching
the floor. The Starting Zone is on the floor in front of the wall.
How it is played
Before each Run, an Official clips the venue's safety line to the Robot's anchor point (Rule 7.4-
3) with a carabiner; the line stays slack, is not part of the Robot, is not measured for height, and
may not assist the climb. The Robot climbs the wall and must be supported by it, not the floor.
Only the highest Band it is stably holding at the final buzzer counts, and a Robot touching the
floor at the buzzer scores 0. Climbing is the whole task; the Robot carries nothing up. If the
highest Band held at the buzzer was first reached during the Autonomous Phase, the Ascent
score is multiplied by 1.5.
